# EDA — Business Entity Resolution

_Stats sections generated by `code/business_entity_resolution/src/eda.py` (seed 42). Key findings at top are hand-written._

## Key findings
1. **Memory is the binding constraint.** 15.7 GB RAM, **no pagefile**, and only ~6.7 GB of commit free once apps were closed. Each S2/S3 file costs ~1.45 GB as pandas str (no pyarrow). Use int64 IDs, categorical country, and chunked reads. Enabling a pagefile is strongly advised.
2. **All-empty baseline ≈ 0.056** (train singleton rate: 5.6% in both US and India). 94% of S1 entities have matches (mean 3.46, median 3, max 11), so recall matters too, not just singleton precision.
3. **Matches come from both sources.** Split is 48% S2 / 52% S3, and 85% of non-singleton entities have matches in both. The mean of ~1.7 matches per source means S2 and S3 each contain duplicates of the same business.
4. **Each S2/S3 ID belongs to at most one S1** (0 exceptions in 7.64M pairs). About 26% of S2/S3 records are never matched (distractors). Enforce one S1 per record at inference.
5. **Country never differs within a matched pair** (0/7.64M). Block within country.
6. **Postal codes are almost absent.** A 5–6 digit number appears in 11–12% of US addresses and ≤1.2% of India/France addresses. Only 8% of US and 0.3% of India pairs share one, so it can't be a primary blocking key.
7. **Blocking-key coverage on matched pairs.** The first non-generic name token agrees in 69% (India) and 80% (US) of pairs. Any address number agrees in 83% and 78%. OR of both gives **95.5% (India)** and **96.5% (US)**, so a union of name-token and address-number/street keys is a good starting point.
8. **Script mismatch in India.** S1 is 100% Latin, but 28% of S2 and 18% of S3 India names are Devanagari (also some Telugu/Kannada). 22% of India pairs have name similarity < 50 for this reason. Train pairs provide Latin↔Devanagari alignments that can be learned from (no external data). A plain `\w+` tokenizer splits Indic words at vowel signs, so keep combining marks (category M).
9. **Hard US/India pairs:** completely different names (DBA/brand: an unrelated brand word against the S1 name) at the same address; empty S2/S3 addresses (~3%) with the same name; house numbers that drift (`1930` ↔ `1715`, `3012` ↔ `3010`).
10. **France (test only), 15% of test S1:** French legal suffixes (SARL/SAS/EURL/SASU/SCI, also `S.A.R.L.`/`S.A.S.U.`), accents (non-ASCII in 16% of S1 names and ~24% of S2/S3), and injected diacritic noise (`Grânde`, `Àmis`, `Çlub`). Addresses are `N Rue …, City, Region` with no postcodes. S2/S3 often use the département (`Gironde`, `Nord`, `Loire-Atlantique`) where S1 uses the region (`Nouvelle-Aquitaine`, `Hauts-de-France`), and use abbreviations/markers (`R.`, `AV`, `IMP`, `N°`/`Nº`, `(6)`, `BIS`/`TER`, `St.-herblain`). Address DF is concentrated in a few cities (Bordeaux 16%, Nantes 14%, Lille 13%), so city-level blocks are large.
11. **Test ratios differ from train.** Test has 5.75 S2+S3 records per S1 vs 4.68 in train, and the country mix changes (India 47% of test S1 vs 40% of train). Expect more distractors or more matches per entity, so don't trust train priors blindly.
12. Files parse cleanly: pandas row counts equal line counts in every file, and `""`-escaped quotes appear in some names.

- 12th Gen i5-12450HX: **8 cores / 12 threads**. RAM **15.7 GB**, no pagefile (commit limit = RAM).
- Free disk: C: 133.6 GB, D: 91.2 GB. Python 3.14.2, pandas 3.0.6 (python-backed str dtype, pyarrow not installed).
- numpy/OpenBLAS failed to allocate its buffers when commit was exhausted. Set `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` when memory is tight.
